# Design method

Establish product truth before style: who is using it, what they must achieve, what information they have, where they are, and what failure would look like. Reuse actual product language. Mark demonstration prices, testimonials and metrics as illustrative; do not invent proof.

Different surfaces have different priorities. A landing page needs a clear promise and evidence that leads to a decision. An operational app needs legible information, predictable navigation and a fast primary task. An OS shell needs discoverability, window behavior and recovery. Carry brand across these surfaces through shared type, color, imagery and detail, without forcing identical layouts.

Create a short direction sheet: two type roles with available fonts, density, spacing rhythm, surface treatment, dominant composition, image crop rules, and one recognizable visual feature. Pick a direction fitting the brief rather than defaulting every product to purple gradients, glass cards and oversized empty heroes. Existing identity wins during refinements.

Plan the screen/state matrix before rendering. Each screen should state its purpose, entry, primary action, content, outcome and recovery. Reuse components only where their behavior truly matches. Make small reusable systems: semantic tokens → controls → patterns → screens. Don't produce hundreds of variants for hypothetical future features.

For visual exploration, show a bounded number of materially different directions only if useful. Once selected, preserve the direction while fixing specific defects. Image generation supplies photography, illustrations, textures or art assets; Figma owns readable text, layout and interaction controls.

Use auto-layout for content relationships, sensible constraints for resizing, editable text with verified fonts and meaningful layer names. Test with a long title, empty result and validation error. Prefer real content over short placeholders that conceal layout failure. Document theme behavior only when themes are in scope.

Check current platform guidance for native patterns when a precise decision depends on it: Apple Human Interface Guidelines (developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines), Material guidance (m3.material.io), Microsoft Windows design (learn.microsoft.com/windows/apps/design). Record chosen device/window sizes. Native app designs are not a claim of native implementation.
